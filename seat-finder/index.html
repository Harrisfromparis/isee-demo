What’s iSEE?

See a place before you visit

iSEE helps you prepare for a visit in the way that suits you. Look up a place, see what it is like, and find your way around it. There is no diagnosis to give, no account to make and nothing to explain.

iSEE is by Autism & Me. It has three parts, and each one works on its own.

  1. Places. Look up a place in any county in Ireland.
  2. What it’s like. Photos and short videos of a place that anyone can share: the way in, the doors, the counter or reception, where you wait, the toilets, the lift and stairs, the lighting and sound, the seating, parking and drop-off. They are shown only after Autism & Me has checked them.
  3. Find your way. Step-by-step routes inside a place, to where you are going: a reception desk, a clinic, a department, a room, a counter or a seat.

Walkthrough

Find your way to where you are going

A big building can be hard to read before you get there. The route part of iSEE lets you preview the way to where you are going, from the front door to a reception desk, a clinic, a department, a room, a counter or a seat. You see what you will pass, what to look for, and where the stairs and lifts are.

You choose how to follow it: one step at a time, or as an overview. Read it, or have a step read aloud when you ask. You can go back, repeat a step, or say you are somewhere else.

Find your way, in three steps

1. Choose where you are going

Choose a place such as a reception desk, a clinic room or a department, or a seat. In the theatre demo, that is the section, row and seat number from a ticket.

2. Choose where you are

Pick the entrance or place you are starting from. Each choice shows the sign to look for.

3. Follow the route

One step at a time, in plain words, with the sign or landmark to look for. You confirm each checkpoint.

System

All three parts of iSEE use what is already there. Places come from open data. What it’s like comes from photos and short videos that people share, and Autism & Me checks each one before it is shown. Find your way uses the building as it is.

For routes, the venue describes its checkpoints and the paths between them. Your phone works out the route. You confirm each checkpoint as you reach it.

4 steps to set up a venue’s routes

  1. Share approved floor and seating plans.
  2. Map existing landmarks, entrances, lifts and rows.
  3. Check routes and step-free status on site.
  4. Publish after the venue approves.

No beacons to install.

Find your way: the diagram in words

  1. Venue map data. The venue is described as checkpoints and the paths between them. Each checkpoint names a sign, lift, door or row marker that is already in the building.
  2. Phone. iSEE finds the shortest path that is allowed, from where you are to where you are going, and shows it as one written step at a time. Sensors would be used only in future phone apps.
  3. You. You confirm each checkpoint as you reach it. You choose the start point and whether the route must be step-free. Confirming works both ways: the phone shows the next step, and you tell it where you are.

A short engineering explanation

  • A venue is a directed graph. Checkpoints are the points. Paths are the one-way lines between them. Each path has its own instruction, because "turn left at the clock" only works in one direction.
  • Routing finds the shortest path. Stairs, lifts, doors and aisles each have a length or a stated cost.
  • Step-free mode never uses stairs. It also never uses a path that has not been checked. Unknown is not treated as step-free.
  • Closed paths are skipped. If a closure appears while you are on a route, iSEE offers to work out a new route. It does not change your route without asking.
  • You confirm each checkpoint. In the web demo, that is how iSEE knows where you are. This website does not use your phone's sensors.
  • The planned phone apps would use the phone's own sensors to help follow your progress. Confirming checkpoints by hand would always stay available. The apps are not available and have not been tested on phones or in venues.
