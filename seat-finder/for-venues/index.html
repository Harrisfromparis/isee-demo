For venues

For venues

iSEE helps everyone see a place before they visit, in the way that suits them: reading, looking at photos, listening, step by step or as an overview, with or without stairs. Visitors give no diagnosis, make no account and explain nothing.

iSEE has three parts: Places, What it’s like and Find your way. A venue can take part in any of them. There are no live venues yet. This page explains what taking part would involve.

For venues

Find your place

Is your place listed? Find it, then tell us you would like an iSEE guide. Places in all 26 counties are listed from open data, and each one shows "No iSEE guide yet".

Find your place

To ask for an iSEE guide for your place, email contactautismandme@gmail.com. You do not need to share any personal details.

For venues

Share your own photos and video

A venue can also share its own photos and short videos of the way in, the doors, the counter or reception, where people wait, the toilets, the lift and stairs, the lighting and sound, the seating, and parking and drop-off. Anyone else can share them too. Autism & Me checks each one before it is shown.

Share what a place is like

For venues

No beacons to buy or install

iSEE needs no electronic beacons. It does not need new QR codes, stickers or markers. It uses what is already in your building: entrances, signs, lifts, concourses, row markers and seat numbers.

For venues

What your venue supplies

  • Approved floor plans, for the route part.
  • Current seating plans or layouts.
  • Your existing landmarks and signage, written as they appear: the words on the sign, where it hangs.
  • Entrances, and the places a visitor may start from.
  • Lifts and stairs, with the step-free status of each one checked by your team.
  • For each section, which way row letters and seat numbers run.
  • Seat features you want to list, such as level-access spaces and the seats beside them.
  • How closures are reported. For example, who marks a lift out of service, and how quickly.
  • Where visitors can talk to venue staff, and the phone number or email for it.
  • If you want to, photos and short videos of the parts of your place that people want to see before they visit.

For venues

What your team approves on site

Before anything is published, an authorised venue representative must walk the routes and approve them on site. That means checking:

  • every route, from each start point to the seats it reaches;
  • the access data, including the step-free status of every lift, ramp, door and aisle;
  • the descriptions of which side each aisle and exit is on;
  • the row and seat numbering direction in every section.

Nothing is published until this is done. A venue guide shows when it was last verified and by which role. A venue that has not been verified is not listed as live.

For venues

What iSEE will not claim

  • iSEE does not certify a building or say that it is accessible.
  • iSEE does not guarantee that a route is open, safe or step-free on the day. Venue staff, signs and emergency procedures come first.
  • iSEE does not say a venue is live until it has been verified on site.
  • iSEE does not claim any venue, partner or organisation endorses it. There are none yet.
  • iSEE does not claim that phone-sensor guidance works. It is planned for the apps and has not been tested on phones or in venues.
